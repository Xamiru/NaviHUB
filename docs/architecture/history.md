## The content rule

Settled with the user on 2026-10-05: **every sentence a reader sees is a verbatim quote from a named source.** No prose is written by NaviHUB or by an agent, and nothing is machine-translated. Quotes in other languages are shown as written; an English translation appears only when a *published* translation exists, as its own cited source.

The schema enforces this structurally. Prose lives only in `Quote.text`. Every other visible string is a name, bibliographic data, or a key from one of the frozen label tables in `schema.ts` (event types, section kinds, relation kinds, roles, link kinds, interpretation topics, position categories, standing labels), which the UI labels. Do not add a free-text prose field to any entity.

Sources are academic books and journals, primary sources, and other encyclopedias and institutions. **Wikipedia and Wikidata are finding aids only**: a research session uses them to find candidates and to follow their footnotes to the real sources, and the validator rejects either host in a cited source or in a quote's provenance. Wikimedia Commons is allowed as the host of images, credited to the holding institution with its license. Research is done by an AI agent, so a publisher that forbids that is never used: OpenStax states its books may not be ingested by large language models (the validator's `no-ai-source` rule rejects `openstax.org`), and themoviedb.org closes its site to Claude agents in robots.txt, so media links take their TMDB ids from Wikidata and carry no TMDB poster or credited character name. The research fetcher refuses any URL whose robots.txt names a Claude or Anthropic agent.

Interpretations record *everything notable, labelled*: scholarly positions, official and national narratives, popular views, revisionist and fringe views. Each position names who holds it and states it in their own quoted words. Its category is always shown. A standing label (mainstream, minority, discredited...) appears only when a quoted source supports it, and fringe and revisionist positions must carry quoted reception, or the validator fails. Contested names are name variants with who uses them, cited.

## Content model

One TypeScript file per entity under `content/<kind>/<slug>.ts`, each `export default defineEvent({...})` (or `definePerson`, `definePeriod`, `definePlace`, `definePolity`, `defineTheme`, `defineSource`, `defineInterpretation`, `defineMedia`). Quotes, claims, sections and course-of-events items live inside the file that owns them. Media files are keyed by the importer identity of a library title (`tmdb-movie-68734`), so curated links resolve against each machine's library by `idx_media_external`.

- **Claims.** Dates, figures and places are `Claim<T>`: one or more sourced alternatives. More than one alternative means the sources disagree, and every value is shown, with who holds it.
- **Dates.** `HistDate.d` is proleptic Gregorian at year, month or day precision, with `approx` ("c.") and `notAfter` (a range of uncertainty). Old Style and Solar Hijri forms are computed for display by `calendars.ts`, never stored: Solar Hijri appears for entities whose regions include `iran`, and Old Style where `julian` is set. The Solar Hijri port follows jalaali-js; dates before Iran adopted the calendar on 31 March 1925 are marked *proleptic*, because the computed date can differ by a day from a commemorated one (5 August 1906 computes to 13 Mordad 1285; Nowruz 1285 fell on 22 March).
- **Slugs** are frozen. `content/ids.lock.json` lists every ref ever committed; the content test fails if one disappears without a redirect, and redirects may not chain. The `my-` prefix is reserved for personal entities.
- **Prominence** (1–3) is curation metadata, not content: it drives semantic zoom on the timeline and the decade lead stories.
- **States** (`polity`, 2026-10-08) are empires, kingdoms, republics, colonies, protectorates and mandates (`POLITY_TYPES`, frozen keys). A state holds dated capitals, the states it succeeded (`predecessors`; successors are derived), the empire it belonged to (`partOf`, dated), its ruling houses (period refs: the Qajar dynasty stays a period beside Qajar Iran), dated area and population figures and `cshapes` links to the map's border units (`set` + `code`, optionally a year span, so a code shared across eras, such as 365 Russia/Soviet Union, splits between states). Rulers are not stored on the state: a person's `Office` carries `polity`, and the state page lists those holders. Events name the states involved through `polities` and a side's `polity`. The state-like periods (German Empire, Austria-Hungary, British Raj, the Soviet Union and twelve more) moved to this kind; `ids.lock.json` redirects `period:x` to `polity:x`, and `historyService` moves this machine's marks, notes, links, archive rows and personal-entity mentions to the new ref once per launch (`historyRepo.followRedirects`), so nothing personal is lost when content is renamed. Old links open the replacement.
- **Further reading** (`furtherReading`, 2026-10-08): the quoted sources are mostly US and UK institutions and Iranica (the agreed core), so events, people, states, periods and themes may list works from other traditions as further reading, `{ source, perspective }` with frozen `PERSPECTIVES` keys (Iranian, Arab, Turkish, Russian and Soviet, Chinese…). They are bibliography only, never quoted or summarised, verified against a catalogue or publisher record; the article shows them under "Further reading", grouped by perspective, without footnote numbers.
- **Themes** (`theme`) are threads across time (oil in Iran, constitutionalism): an ordered `thread` of events, people, states and periods, each optionally with a quote on why it belongs and a dated claim, plus framing sections. Every page lists the themes it belongs to.
- **Quality lint** (`src/shared/history/lint.ts`): the research skill's Quality bar as checks (non-English or mid-story openers, chronology openers, citation tails, scan damage, unlinked participants and state sides, Iranians without a Persian name, thin lead events, people without dates, repeats on a page, one image on more than three pages, sections that open mid-story, state propaganda outlets cited outside their government's claim, and government claims without an independent assessment, quotes cut from inside a sentence, interpretation quotes included, paragraph locators that are not numbers, titles in other languages that cite no source, a day paired with a coarser form of itself (`false-dispute`), leading events with no located place (`unpinned`), primary sources narrating an event (`primary-narration`), and leading pages opening on a pre-1950 work (`dated-opener`)). `tools/check_quotes.py` is the matching guard for the research tools: it re-finds every committed quote in its cached page and fails on any page whose paragraph count changed. The `official` position category is labelled "Government claim" (2026-10-09): a ruler's or state's words are a claim, never a neutral account. `tests/historyContent.test.ts` holds the catalog to `content/lint.baseline.json`: issues that existed when the lint arrived stay listed until fixed, anything new fails, and a fixed issue left in the baseline fails too, so the baseline only shrinks (`tools/lint_report.ts --write-baseline` after fixing).

## The catalog and the index

`catalog.ts` collects the content with Vite's compile-time `import.meta.glob`, so research sessions add files and never maintain an index. It is imported **only** by `historyService.ts`, which ipc.ts and searchRepo load through a cached dynamic `import()`; the content never parses at launch, and nothing in the renderer may import it (`tests/performanceBoundaries.test.ts`). The build puts it in its own chunk.

The service builds a pure in-memory index (`historyIndex.ts`) from the catalog plus `history_user_entity` rows: timeline entries, part-of children, inbound relations, people's events, interpretations by subject, media links by target and by portrayed person, citation counts and search documents. Personal entities invalidate it on save. The page payloads (`historyViews.ts`) are pure functions of the index plus a `ViewContext` (marks, library lookups, archive rows, a cached-image lookup), so tests feed fixtures with no database.

Global search merges History hits through `searchRepo.globalAll()` (the `search:global` handler); the synchronous `global()` keeps returning `history: []`. The normaliser folds diacritics, Arabic and Persian letter variants, joiners and Persian digits, and compares Persian compounds with and without the joiner or space.

## Pages

- **Landing** (`/history`): a mouse-first world timeline stage (`HistoryTimeline.tsx`), a fixed night-sky island styled by the `.history-stage` block in `styles.css` (one `--hist-<region>` hue per stream). Each region is a coloured stream (Iran pinned first) with its periods as ribbons; global periods are tall bands; long events are glowing capsules (named inside when they fit), lead events picture medallions once their image is cached, the rest dots. The viewport is about two thirds of the window: the wheel zooms around the pointer and hands back to page scroll once fully zoomed out, Alt+wheel or a vertical drag moves across the regions, a horizontal drag pans with momentum, double-click dives in (on an event, opens its page), and the scrubber's window drags and resizes. A cursor line reads the year and a hover card shows the event's picture, type, region and dates. The region chips jump to a stream. Keyboard users focus the stage (arrows pan, plus and minus zoom); The List view is the same data as a decade-grouped table for keyboard and screen-reader use. Selecting an event fills a lens and sets the pinned page background to its archival image, the franchise-page treatment. Below it, decade front pages: lead stories, the rest by region, events continuing from earlier decades, born and died, and linked library titles (including those of continuing events).
- **Articles** (`/history/{event,person,period,place,polity,theme}/:id`): a hero over the pinned background, a sticky contents rail, the quoted text, course of events, sub-events, before and after, interpretations, people by side, meanwhile elsewhere or contemporaries, in media (with fact-vs-fiction quotes, portrayals resolved to the library's credited actors, and Import for missing titles), archive, the numbered bibliography and a private note. Footnote markers are numbered by first citation of each source on the page; the popover shows the reference, locator and how the text was copied. Facts sit in a sticky infobox at `xl` and move inline above the article below it.
- **States** read as articles with founded/ended, capitals, predecessors and successors, the empire they belonged to, ruling houses and figures in the infobox, then rulers and officials (from person offices) under a succession chart (`@shared/history/succession.ts`: one row per office, titles folded by `officeKey` so "Shah of Iran" and "shah of Iran" share a row, only offices that passed between two dated holders, six rows at most; year-precision handoffs read as succession, a disputed or approximate edge is drawn soft, an open end runs to the next holder or the state's end; holders with no start stay in the list only; the chart is `aria-hidden` and a screen-reader table carries the same reigns), a territory strip of the state's outline in every border version it links to (computed in main from the map's borders, loaded lazily only for a state page with map links), its events in time order, and its colonies and dependencies. States also ride the timeline as ribbons, like periods.
- **Themes** (`/history/themes`, `/history/theme/:id`): the thread as one cross-century timeline.
- **On this day** is a Home widget (`historyToday`): events with a day-precise start, end or course date on today's month and day (`history:onThisDay`). It waits a few seconds after Home opens, because its first call loads the History catalog in main.
- **World in a year** (`/history/year/:year`, astronomical years, `history:year`, `views.yearView`): events under way by region, periods, states standing (a state with no end date still stands, though the timeline stores it as a point) with the office holders in post that year (an office with no end counts only in the year it began), births and deaths, and the Lunar and Solar Hijri years the year overlaps, counted from the calendar its stored days use (Julian before 1582). Reached from the map's year label, the infobox start dates ("The world in …") and On this day's years. No text of its own.
- **Source age**: `@shared/history/sourceAge.ts`. A citation from scholarship or a reference work dated before 1950 carries an "older scholarship" badge, and a page whose narrative quotes rest mostly on such works says so beside its Sources heading (`datedNarration`).
- **Quiz** (`/history/quiz`, `/history/quiz/:slug`; a Topbar tab and a group on `/quiz`): four accuracy-ranked kinds dealt in main by `historyQuizPool.ts` through the pure builders in `@shared/history/quiz.ts`, from read pages by default or every page. Chronology puts five events of one era in order; Year offers four years spaced by era (none in the future); Who said it uses a person page's "In their own words" quotes and views held by one person in a primary text, skips passages that name their speaker and shows the source once answered; Whose land asks which border unit held an event's located place in its year, only when the same unit holds it the year before and after (the map kind loads the full borders). Disputed or approximate years and titles that contain a year are never asked. Rounds log through `quiz:logSession` with one `finish()` funnel.
- **Sources** (`/history/sources`, `/history/source/:id`), **My additions** (`/history/my`), the personal editor (`/history/new/:kind`, `/history/my/:id/edit`) and **Corrections** (`/history/corrections`).
- Library detail pages show a History fact from `history:backlinks`. `historyBacklinkGate.ts` answers it first from the slug lock's `media:` refs and the user's personal links, so a title with neither never loads the catalog.

## Personal layer

Per machine, in `navihub.db`: `history_mark` (read date, favorite), `history_note` (kind `note` or `correction`), `history_media_link` (the user's own title links, cascading with the title), `history_archive` (files under the `history/` prefix, one row per file, `suggestion_key` = `<ref>#<suggestion id>` for research downloads), `history_user_entity` (personal entities as the content schema's JSON) and `history_request` (pages the user looked up and did not find: free text plus an optional note and a done date). All are wiped from shared exports. Content refs are strings, so these tables have no foreign key to the content; a ref whose entity is gone is ignored.

The in-app editor writes personal entities that pass the same validator as researched content. Corrections to researched content go through research sessions: a page note marked *correction* collects on the Corrections page, which copies them for the next session. **Requests** (2026-10-18) ride the same page: Ctrl+K offers "Request a History page" when a search of three or more characters finds no History page, the Requests tab adds, lists and marks them done, and the copy button appends the open ones under "Requested pages:". `views.resolveRequest` links a request to a page that has the request as one of its names or transliterations, compared after search normalisation (2026-10-21: a prefix no longer counts, so "Iran" never resolves to the Iranian Revolution nor "Roman Empire" to the Holy Roman Empire, and a source's title or author is never a page); anything else stays open until the user marks it done.

## Archive

`history.dir` (default `userData/history`) holds attached and downloaded files, one folder per entity (`event-1953-iranian-coup/`). It changes only through Settings → Folders → Move… (`storageMove`, like `media.dir` and `pictures.dir`), and deleting a personal entry deletes its archive rows and copies. Attaching copies the picked file in (NaviHUB owns these copies, unlike Football's in-place attachments) with a SHA-256; research suggestions download over https with per-kind caps through `streamResponseToFile`. Both are tasks (`historyAttach`, `historyDownload`) with status polled through `history:archiveJobs`, and `cancelHistoryJobs()` sits in the before-quit registry right after `cancelActiveFootballSync()`. Recordings play in the global player under the `history-` track namespace (no Like, no Favorite, no play logging); video and documents open in the system's apps; photos open in the lightbox.

Curated hero images, portraits and posters are cached into the `media/` pool by `historyImages.ts`, the franchise art pattern, with a descriptive User-Agent that Wikimedia's servers require. Wikimedia rate-limits bursts, so there is one paced queue: a single download at a time, `MIN_GAP_MS` apart, a 429 waited out inside `fetchWithRetry` (pausing the whole queue), the page being viewed first and every timeline event's picture queued behind it as a background prefetch, lead events first (about six minutes for the whole catalog on a first run; a quit cancels it and the next visit resumes). The renderer never loads a remote image URL itself (`historyImageSrc` returns only the local copy, a placeholder until then) and refreshes the views at most every `LIVE_IMAGE_REFRESH_MS` (10 s) while a batch runs, plus once when it settles, because each refresh rebuilds the open views in main. A URL that fails waits `RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS` before it is queued again, doubling with each further failure up to six hours, so a dead link does not restart the prefetch task all session. Every Wikimedia request also takes a slot from the process-wide `wikimediaThrottle` in `fetchWithRetry`, shared with Football, wrestling and music art.

## Research sessions

The `history-era` skill is the ritual: find candidates through finding aids, read real sources, copy quotes verbatim with locator and provenance, write entity files with frozen slugs and lock entries, add archive suggestions with credit and license, and finish with `npm run typecheck` plus `npm run test:main -- tests/historyContent.test.ts` green. Its `tools/` (Python, standard library) cut every quote from a cached copy of the page by start and end phrases, so no quote is typed by hand, and refuse to overwrite a content file the session did not create. The pilot is the 20th century, decade by decade from the 1900s, then the 19th century from the 1800s (added 2026-10-06), about 20–25 events per decade on the first pass, world coverage with extra depth on Iran. Since 2026-10-18 the skill also has a **dossier** mode for lookups: one subject the user requested (a state, person, period or theme) gets its page, its 10 to 20 major events, its rulers or office holders and the links into existing pages, through the same sources-first list, spec-only research, draft review and single builder. Subjects outside the Iranian spine (Rome, Benin) enter the catalog this way rather than through the era plan. A person's "appears in" list reads in event-start order (`historyIndex.ts` sorts `personEvents`; undated events last), so a dossier's events read as a chronology on the person page.

## Map

`/history/map` (`HistoryMapPage.tsx`) draws the world's borders in any year with each event pinned at its first place that has coordinates.

- **Borders** come from CShapes 2.0 (world, 1886–2019) and CShapes-Europe (from 1816), both CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 by Schvitz et al., ETH Zürich, and, for 1700–1885 outside Europe (and within it before 1816), from Cliopatria (Seshat Global History Databank; Bennett et al., *Scientific Data* 2025; CC BY 4.0), whose hand-drawn atlas outlines are approximate (known errors include Turkmen-steppe dates, Herat and Talysh). `build-history-borders.cjs --early <cliopatria.geojson>` adds that layer (`set: 'early'`) to the existing file without the CShapes downloads; polities that continue past 1886 take their CShapes code (Persia 630, Ottoman 640, Russia 365, Afghanistan 700, Oman 698) so colour and links carry across the seam, the rest their Wikidata number. Before 1886 the map draws the early layer beneath CShapes-Europe and says the outlines are approximate; a state page links early units through `cshapes` entries with `set: 'early'`. `scripts/build-history-borders.cjs` turns the two GeoJSON downloads into `src/main/history/data/borders.json`: outer rings only, Douglas–Peucker simplified, quantized to 0.01° and delta-encoded, each distinct outline stored once and referenced by every border version that shares it. Rerun it when CShapes publishes a new version.
- **Loading**: `historyMap.ts` imports the file as raw text (`?raw`, then `JSON.parse`) on first use, through a lazy `import()` in `ipc.ts`, so neither launch nor the History catalog pays for it (`tests/performanceBoundaries.test.ts`). The renderer receives the whole set once and scrubs years without further IPC.
- **Geometry** is pure in `src/shared/history/mapGeometry.ts`: the Equal Earth projection, outline decoding, and `layersAt(year)` — the world set from 1886; before that, Europe's borders over a borderless silhouette of the 1886 world, which the page says in words. A state's colour comes from its code, so it holds across border versions; Iran uses its timeline hue.
- **Pins** (`views.mapPins`) need a place with `coords`. Coordinates are cited data like any other: the research tools' `gazetteer.py` takes them from Natural Earth's public-domain populated places (by name and ISO country, never by name alone), and the decade builds apply it to every place without coordinates. Towns and villages Natural Earth lacks come from GeoNames cities500 (CC BY 4.0, credited under the map), matched by name or alternate name and country; a battlefield or site takes the position of the town it is named after, and the citation names the town. Regions, waters and the remaining sites stay unpinned until a source supplies them. CShapes-Europe's first full year is 1816 (earlier it records only Liechtenstein and San Marino), so Europe before 1816 also comes from the early layer.
- **Interaction** follows the timeline: wheel zoom around the pointer (handing back to page scroll when fully out), drag to pan, double-click to dive in, hover a state for its name, border years and capital, hover a pin for its card, click a pin to open the event. A state whose border unit links to a state page names that page in its hover card and opens it on a click (after the double-click window, so a double-click still zooms); the same states are listed under the map as links for keyboard users (`history:mapPolities`, `?year=` opens a given year). The year slider scrubs and plays; an event page with a located place offers "On the map" (`/history/map?focus=<ref>`), which opens at its year, centred on a ringed pin.

## Before 1700 (2026-10-13)

- **Front pages** (`src/shared/history/buckets.ts`): a decade from 1700, a century from 1000 to 1699, a half-millennium before 1000 CE, BCE counted as readers count it ("1000–501 BCE"). `bucketOf(year)` is the one rule; the overview, the front page (`history:decade` takes any year inside the page) and its image prefetch all use it, and `bucketLabel` writes the pill and heading.
- **Dates** (`calendars.ts`, `HistDateText`): years before 1 CE read "480 BCE". A day before the Gregorian reform (15 October 1582) is stored in the Julian calendar, as historians and the sources give it (schema.ts HistDate), and reads "(Julian)" with its computed proleptic Gregorian form beneath; Hijri forms are computed from that converted day. Iran and Middle East pages (`view.lunarHijri`) add the tabular lunar Hijri date from the Hijra to 31 March 1925, labelled "(tabular)" because sighted-moon dates can differ by a day or two; Solar Hijri stays as before. All of it is computed for display only; content stores the Julian day before the reform and the Gregorian day after it (a later Old Style source keeps `julian: true` on a converted Gregorian day).
- **Map**: `build-history-borders.cjs --ancient <cliopatria.geojson>` writes everything Cliopatria draws before 1700 to `data/borders-ancient.json` and records `ancientFrom` in `borders.json`, so the slider reaches back to 3400 BCE before the file loads. The map page fetches it the first time the year goes below 1700 and merges it with `withAncient` (shape numbers offset), so colours, labels and state links work across the seam; a state page whose start is before 1700 gets its territory from the merged data. Units in both files use `set: 'early'` and Cliopatria's codes.
- **Content**: royal inscriptions, decrees and court chronicles are rulers' claims under the existing rule (`official` positions with independent reception, never narration); research before 1700 goes by era, Iran as the spine (the plan in the research work folder, ERAS-PLAN.md).
