## The JLPT ladder

**Levels as progress, never a gate (2026-08-16).** `@shared/jlptLevels.ts` owns the rules; `japaneseRepo.jlptLadder()` is one pass over `jp_card ⋈ jp_lesson ⋈ jp_course`; `components/japanese/JlptLadder.tsx` renders it in a "JLPT ladder" section on the roadmap page.

**No schema change.** Courses already carry a free-text level LABEL (`'N5'`, `'N5+'`, `'N4–N3'`, `'N3–N2'` — what the seeds write and the course pages show). `parseJlptLevel` takes the FIRST tier a label names: the tier the course is pitched at, with the range's upper end treated as stretch material. Higher-numbered N is easier, so "first mentioned" is also "lowest". A label naming no tier yields null, and those cards are reported as `unlevelled` rather than dropped — a total the user cannot reconcile with their library is worse than a caveat.

**"Passed" is defined in this app's terms.** The SRS here is Anki-shaped (`new`/`learning`/`review`/`relearn` + `interval_days`), not WaniKani's stage ladder, so Guru has no direct equivalent. A card counts once it is `status='review'` with `interval_days >= PASSED_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7) — it has left the learning steps and earned a real interval. The UI states that definition in its footnote instead of borrowing WaniKani's word without its machinery.

A level clears at `LEVEL_PASS_RATIO` (90%), the user's choice, so a few stubborn cards cannot stall it. `currentLevel` is the easiest uncleared level, **skipping levels with no cards** — a library that starts at N3 must not read as "you are on N5 forever" — and settles on the hardest level with cards once everything is cleared.

**Nothing locks.** The user decided levels track progress and gate nothing: this section is their only Japanese source, so it must never be the reason a card is unavailable. `tests/jlptLadder.test.ts` pins both the pure rules and the real SQL.

## Adaptive daily study and guided listening

**Tutor pass (2026-08-24).** The Japanese home is now organized around one daily loop rather than a catalogue of 24 equal-weight tools. `@shared/japanese/dailyPlan.ts` turns the review setup's new-card choice into a true daily budget using `JpStats.introducedToday`: opening a second review session can clear more due cards but cannot introduce another full batch. The target persists in `japanese.dailyNewTarget`, so Home and fresh Review visits agree. An unseen-card backlog advises holding the next lesson, with an explicit override because the section never locks content. Home pairs recall with guided listening and current manga/novel immersion, then collapses the full catalogue into four toolbox categories. The Home manga query remains byte-identical `{ mediaType: 'manga' }` so it still shares `qk.media.home` with Home and `RoadmapDailyLoop`.

**Home hierarchy hardening (2026-08-31).** Home waits for its local stats, roadmap, settings and manga queries before displaying counts or an empty state, and offers a retry if any projection fails. The daily loop precedes the course map. `@shared/japanese/courseWindow.ts` keeps the five visible course nodes centred on the live frontier instead of permanently showing Steps 1-5, while the full roadmap remains one click away. The toolbox category switcher uses the shared accessible Tabs idiom rather than four oversized selection cards.

`japanese:listeningPool` (`src/main/jpListening.ts`) reuses the installed Tatoeba sentence-audio pack and the i+1 feed's two-pass known-word classifier. A plain awaited Start request returns randomized recordings containing either zero unknown words or exactly one; no network is used and no randomized pool is cached. `/japanese/listen` defaults to guided listening: audio and meaning choice come before the transcript, then the learner shadows and can compare an in-memory mic take with the native recording. Pronunciation is deliberately self-compared, not falsely auto-graded. Dictation remains the second tab and keeps its own `dictation` history; guided rounds log `listening`.

End-of-lesson checks now follow recognition with up to three English-to-Japanese production prompts from vetted lesson sentences (`@shared/japanese/lessonProduction.ts`). The model is revealed only for self-comparison because equivalent Japanese wording can be valid; this layer never writes SRS grades. Grammar-library points stage as one small unlearned lesson per point (`N5 · ている`) and enter reviews only after the learner reads and marks that lesson learned. The old whole-level IPC remains for compatibility but is no longer exposed by the grammar page.

## Tutor roadmap, sound, output and long-form listening

**Tutor system (2026-08-30).** `/japanese/tutor` is the learner's operating page, not another static catalogue. `@shared/japanese/tutor.ts` maps the 26 seeded steps onto five phases (foundation, controlled-reading bridge, immersion bridge, independent comprehension and advanced range), then builds a sixty-minute prescription from the live due/unseen load, daily new-card budget, course frontier, strict 30-day retention, recent skill histories and attached local media. The fixed balance is 15 minutes recall, 10 listening, 20 reading, 10 controlled output and 5 lesson-or-repair. Missing or weak evidence names one remediation target; an unseen backlog recommends holding the next lesson. Both are recommendations only — no route or content is locked. The page also states weekly proof and exit evidence for every phase so the full route to independent manga, visual novels and anime is visible in one place.

The tutor adds no schema and no new IPC. Evidence rides the existing `quiz_session` table through `quiz:logSession` / `quiz:history`: `jpPhonology`, `jpOutput` and `jpImmersion` are Japanese quiz kinds and therefore also belong in `JP_QUIZ_KINDS`. Reading curriculum uses the next unread attached manga/EPUB chapter when one exists, then an in-progress visual novel as the real-media fallback; listening curriculum uses a locally attached anime video. Otherwise the prescription falls back to graded reading and the existing known-word guided listening pool.

**Guided Tutor sessions** (`/japanese/tutor/session`) turn that prescription into a persistent daily docket stored in `japanese.tutorSession.v1`. Starting captures a baseline; review counts, introduced cards, quiz histories and local chapter timestamps then complete observable blocks automatically. Completion uses minimum evidence rather than any token activity: recall requires up to ten post-start reviews (or a cleared queue), guided listening needs five prompts, sound three questions, reading four questions, output six points, and long-form listening at least three passes plus a forty-character retell. Unlogged speaking, reading and valid variations can still be marked manually and are labelled as such. A same-window event keeps the shell-wide return strip synchronized. A post-start scored result below the skill threshold inserts an immediate five-minute repair, which can always be dismissed.

Closing a session upserts one personal `jp_tutor_day` row and automatically files weak scored results in `jp_tutor_error`; the Tutor page also accepts learner-reported problems and keeps each row open until resolved. The debrief labels its duration honestly as **planned minutes completed**, never elapsed or evidenced time. `@shared/japanese/tutorSession.ts` owns the deterministic live progress/repair rules; `japaneseRepo` owns the durable history through `japanese:saveTutorDebrief`, `:tutorDays`, `:addTutorError`, `:tutorErrors` and `:resolveTutorError`. Both tables are wiped from sanitized exports. There is still no interval, model or network dependency.

**Sound foundation** (`/japanese/phonology`) is eight authored offline units in `@shared/japanese/phonology.ts`: mora timing, long vowels, small っ, moraic ん, devoicing, particle pronunciations, pitch and connected speech. Examples expose explicit mora divisions and the renderer generates an even beat with Web Audio; no audio download or network call is involved. The three-question checks measure sound-system decisions. Copy remains explicit that generated timing and self-comparison do not grade the learner's accent.

**Controlled output** (`/japanese/output`) is ten three-prompt units in `@shared/japanese/output.ts`, moving from sentence construction through tense/aspect transformation, conversational repair, role-play, scene retelling and reasoned N2–N1 writing. The learner produces before revealing a model, repairs the response and self-rates independence. `checkOutput` is deliberately narrow and deterministic: it normalizes text and reports whether vetted structural signals appear. It never claims that a different valid sentence is wrong or that a matching sentence is fully natural. A microphone take can be recorded and replayed in memory through the existing `usePitchRecorder`; nothing is uploaded or persisted.

**Branching role-play** (`/japanese/roleplay`) provides twelve authored, multi-turn conversations in `@shared/japanese/roleplay.ts`, ranging from everyday repairs and clinic/station interactions through workplace negotiation, interviews, academic disagreement and client recovery. The learner produces before seeing the vetted response paths; the selected path controls the next authored partner turn. Structure matching reports only the required conversational functions. Completion logs `jpOutput` with `settings.mode='branching-roleplay'`; the next-practice action reads those scenario ids and chooses an unseen or least-recent conversation so short-term repetition is delayed without a new quiz kind.

**Long-form listening** (`/japanese/immersion`) turns locally attached anime video into a five-stage ladder: known two-minute scene, five-minute sequence, ten-minute scene, full episode with Japanese subtitles and full cold episode. Every session uses the same five-pass protocol — cold, Japanese subtitles, focused transcript, shadow selected lines, retell from memory — and logs the completed passes, retell length, exact range and a 1–5 comprehension self-check. `@shared/videoStudyRange.ts` validates a maximum four-hour range and formats a copyable `Start X, stop Y` cue. Because playback belongs to the operating system's default application, NaviHUB never claims control: the active workspace keeps that cue visible, tells the learner to pause at the boundary after each pass, and states that the external player remains unrestricted. Protocol state survives navigation through `usePersistedState`; incomplete protocols may still be logged, but they do not complete the Tutor block until the evidence minimum is met.

**Renderer hardening (2026-08-30).** Every Japanese route is a `React.lazy` boundary under App's shared `Suspense` fallback, keeping the vertical's large corpora and drill code out of initial startup. Japanese form controls carry programmatic labels (including the on-screen-keyboard wrapper), captured review frames have descriptive alt text, sentence playback uses the shared SVG transport icon, and feedback colors use the theme-owned `signal-affirmative/caution/anomaly` roles rather than literal green/amber/red utilities.

**UI audit hardening (2026-08-31).** Selected quiz/setup pills and multi-select chips expose their state through `aria-pressed`; bespoke review and dictionary outcomes use live status text; meaningful instructions never use the decorative `gray-600` tier. The Tutor error ledger initially shows seven rows but can reveal every open problem, so every durable error remains resolvable. Lesson vocabulary/kanji tables are labelled keyboard-scroll regions under zoom or narrow widths. The Guide has imperative in-page navigation (never HashRouter `href="#…"`) and its role-play/player copy tracks the current twelve-scenario, system-player design.

**Graded reading expansion.** `@shared/japanese/readings/` now holds 42 connected passages across N5–N1: six original passages plus three additional passages at each N5–N2 level, and six N1 passages. The existing furigana-content guard, glossary membership checks, question-language rule and answer-bias checks cover the expanded corpus. N1 uses the same four-question, per-passage-history contract as every other level.

## On-screen Japanese keyboard

**On-screen Japanese keyboard (2026-08-12)** — `JpKeyboardInput` (`components/japanese/keyboard/`) is a drop-in for `<input className="input">` at JP-typing sites (v1: dictionary search, shiritori, dictation — deferred: mining/lesson-form fields, textareas): an あ toggle opens a panel with three switchable layouts (gojūon chart rendered COLUMNS right-to-left あ-rightmost, flick 12-key with drag petals in `FlickKey.tsx`, romaji QWERTY) + an IME candidate bar. ## Wave 4 — sentence games, arcade, an honest JLPT test, graded reading

**Japanese wave 4 (2026-08-15)** — three additions that generate their own content, one that stops lying, and two new seeded courses. **Foundations**: `JpToken.posDetail` (kuromoji `pos_detail_1`, `null` for `*` — the one construction site is `tokenizer.ts`); QuizKind += `particles scramble contextReading kanaRace readingRace conjRace jpReading`, all added to `JP_QUIZ_KINDS` (now exported and guarded by NEW `tests/jpQuizKindsSync.test.ts`, which reads the kind literals under `pages/Japanese*` + `components/japanese/` as TEXT and diffs them against the list, filtered through the QuizKind union so a lesson `kind: 'cards'` never trips it); `quizRepo.SCORE_RANKED_KINDS` (the three races) makes `history()` rank the record by **score** rather than accuracy — a time attack's record is most-correct-in-the-minute, and ranking by ratio would reward slow careful play (Shiritori's score=total hack predates this); `api.quiz.history(kind, limit?)` + `qk.quiz.historyAll(kind)` (under the `history(kind)` prefix, so one invalidation refreshes both) exist for the per-passage bests below. `@shared/kanaRows.ts` and `@shared/dojoWords.ts` lift the kana row tables and the conjugation dojo words out of `JapaneseKanaPage` verbatim so the arcade can share them; `dojoWords.ts` adds pure `posToWordClass(pos, front)` mapping a seeded card's `pos` onto a conjugation class.

**Sentence games** (`/japanese/sentences`, tabs particles | scramble | reading, `?tab=` seeded): pure model in NEW `@shared/japanese/sentenceGames.ts` — `eligibleParticles` (whitelisted 助詞, never the sentence-final one, never adjacent to another particle so には/では stay whole), `blankAt`, `particleOptions` with **`PARTICLE_CONFLICTS`**: a distractor that would often ALSO be correct (が for は, も for either, へ for に) is never offered, which is the honest answer to は/が — exclusion, not accept-both, which would hand over a free elimination; `chunkTokens` (bunsetsu rule: 接頭詞 binds forward, 助詞/助動詞/記号 and posDetail 接尾/非自立 attach backwards, 名詞+名詞 merges unless the first is 代名詞); `stripFinalPunct`; `contextCandidates` (kanji-bearing, reading present, 名詞 minus 数/接尾/非自立 or a dictionary-form 動詞/形容詞 — so the JMdict check is exact). Pools in NEW `src/main/jpSentenceGames.ts` over NEW `dict/sentences.ts:sampleSentences({minChars,maxChars,limit})`; `contextReadingPool` accepts a word ONLY when kuromoji's reading is among JMdict's for that expression (`d.priority >= 0`, direct SQL — `lookupWord` would fall back to jisho.org over the network, which this section may never do), and returns every attested reading so 魚 takes さかな and うお alike. Channels `japanese:particlePool | scramblePool | contextReadingPool`; renderer `JapaneseSentencesPage` + `ParticleDrill` (McDrill, 4-up options, audio when the pack has the clip) / `ScrambleDrill` (own loop — McDrill is single-pick: tray → answer row, keys 1-N, Backspace undoes, Enter checks; copy is explicit that it grades against the ORIGINAL order and yours may still be grammatical) / `ContextReadingDrill` (TypedDrill with a `node` prompt highlighting the target). Tests: `jpSentenceGames` (hand-authored token arrays), `jpSentencePools` (real dict schema, tokenizer mocked by a fixture map).

**Arcade** (`/japanese/arcade`, tabs kana | reading | conj): NEW `components/japanese/ArcadeShell.tsx` owns the clock (deadline + a 200 ms ticker, red under 10 s), the score/streak header and two input loops — `keystroke` (the DJT loop via `matchState`: a match advances with no key press, a dead end reveals in place) for kana and readings, `enter` (via `readingMatches`) for conjugation, where answers are long and a typo should not cost the clock twice. Logs score = correct, total = attempted, `settings {seconds: 60, …}`. Reading race pool = NEW `jpDrills.readingRacePool` (learned cards first, topped up from `freq × term` under rank 8000).

**JLPT test rebuilt** (`/japanese/test`): NEW `src/main/jpJlpt.ts` `jlptTestPool({level})` builds four sections from the PACKS — grammar cloze from the levelled `grammar_point` bank, vocabulary from `JLPT_RANK_WINDOWS` × JMdict (JMdict has no JLPT tags, so the level is a **frequency proxy** and the section carries that note), kanji readings restricted to KANJIDIC-levelled characters at or below the level (exported `kanjiByLevel`; absent pack → an "unlevelled" note rather than a silent lie), and reading = a bank sentence in a per-level length band with three other translations as distractors. Pure `rebalanceSizes()` hands an unavailable section's slots round-robin to the rest so the length holds. `null` = packs missing, and the page keeps its original seeded-course sampler as the fallback — tagged `source: 'courses'`, with copy saying it measures this app's curriculum, not the level. The verdict now prints per-section scores and states plainly that thirty items certify nothing. `settings {level, source, sections}`. Tests: `tests/jpJlpt.test.ts` over the real dict schema.

**Graded reading** (`/japanese/reading`): NEW `@shared/japanese/types.ts` + `furigana.ts` — notation `漢字[かんじ]`, the bracket attaching to the maximal preceding kanji run, with `parseFurigana`/`stripFurigana`/`furiganaProblems`; the last is the content gate (`tests/jpReadings.test.ts`), and its strictest rule is that EVERY kanji run must carry a reading, because the furigana toggle is the learner's control and a half-annotated passage breaks it. Content in `@shared/japanese/readings/{n5,n4,n3,n2}.ts` + `index.ts` (24 passages, 6 per level; prompts English at N5/N4 and Japanese from N3 up). `components/japanese/Furigana.tsx` renders `<ruby>` and hides `<rt>` with `invisible` so the line height never jumps on toggle. The page mirrors `EnglishReadingPage` with a level filter, a persisted furigana toggle, `MiningPanel` on double-click of a paragraph, and a per-passage "best n/4" chip read from `history('jpReading', 200)` matched on `settings.passageKey` — four-question rounds never reach the `total >= 5` personal-best rule, so those chips ARE the record and there is no QuizRecord block.

**Two seeded courses** in NEW `src/main/db/japaneseSeed4.ts` (japaneseSeed.ts is 4k lines): `Anime & Manga Set Phrases` (step 5, 9 lessons / 108 cards — doorway lines, meals and thanks, apologies, battle talk, reactions, refusals, comfort, partings, manga connectives) and `VN, Gaming & Net Slang` (step 12, 10 lessons / 123 cards — save/config menus, routes and endings, tropes, RPG systems, gacha, grinding, online play, net slang, streaming, reviews). Both were authored against the existing catalog: a NEW pack may not reuse a front another course already has (two SRS cards for one word, and it shows up twice in every drill picker), which `tests/japaneseSeed.test.ts` now pins for these two decks — the catalog at large keeps its ~117 deliberate vocabulary/kanji overlaps. `ALL_PACKS` gained `japanese.seeded.setphrases` and `.vnslang`; every later pack's `difficulty` moved up (Counters 6 … N1 Kanji 26) and `STEP_REORDER3`/`reorderSteps3` (flag `japanese.seeded.order4`, reverse walk, matched by ORIGINAL title + OLD step) renumbers a live 24-step DB before seeding. `tests/japaneseSeed.test.ts` gained a full 24→26 replay asserting only the two new rows are fresh.
