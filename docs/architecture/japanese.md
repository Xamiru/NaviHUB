## Built-in course content sync (2026-10-16)

Before this, `seedPack` inserted each built-in course once behind a settings flag and nothing could ever reach a library that already had it: a corrected reading or a new lesson helped fresh installs only. Every built-in row now carries a frozen `seed_key` and a `seed_hash` (`jp_course`/`jp_lesson`/`jp_card`, ensureColumn'd, indexes in `runMigrations`).

- **Keys.** Course = the pack key in `ALL_PACKS` (`n5`, `radicals`, …); lesson = the explicit `key` on each `SeedLesson` (`n5.01`); card = `<lesson key>/<card key ?? front>`. `japaneseSeedKeys.lock.json` holds every shipped key; `tests/japaneseSeedSync.test.ts` fails on a vanished or unlocked key. Correcting a card's front means setting `key: '<old front>'` so the existing row keeps its review history.
- **Three-way rule** (`japaneseSeedSync.ts`). `seed_hash` is the hash of the seed content last written to the row, over exactly the user-editable columns. A row whose current content still hashes to it is unedited and gets rewritten; one that differs is the user's edit and is left alone; one that already equals today's seed just adopts the hash. Updates write content columns only, never SRS state. New lessons/cards land after their previous seed sibling (later `sort_order` shifted, the in-memory snapshot shifted with it). A seed row the seed dropped is deleted only while unedited, unreviewed (no `jp_review_log` row, since a reset card keeps its log), free of mined audio/frame/source and, for a lesson, not marked learned. A user-deleted lesson or card is tombstoned (`source='user'`) by `removeLesson`/`removeCard`; a deleted course needs no tombstone because the sync skips any pack whose course row is gone.
- **Backfill** (once, `japanese.seeded.keys`). Matches each flagged pack's rows one-to-one by course title (plus `formerTitles`; a course the user renamed is found by holding at least half the pack's lesson titles), lesson kind+title (plus `formerTitles`) and card front (plus `key`). A row is trusted only when it equals today's seed (or the seed under a former title); anything else gets a NULL hash, because an older seed text and a user edit are indistinguishable. Seed lessons/cards with no row get a `source='backfill'` tombstone: a deletion and content added to the pack after this library seeded it are indistinguishable too. On the laptop library on 2026-10-16 this matched all 2,360 cards, found nothing missing and left 4 N4 grammar lessons (old bodies) untrusted.
- **Restore.** Settings → Learning → "Built-in course updates" shows `seedDrift()` (`japanese:builtInStatus`): built-in rows the sync leaves alone (NULL hash or edited since) and backfill tombstones. After a confirm, `japanese:restoreBuiltIn` → `reattachSeedRows` makes every built-in row's hash its current content, lifts the backfill tombstones (never the user's own), clears the version stamp and reruns the sync, rejecting on failure so the renderer never shows a false success. Review progress, user-made rows and in-app deletions survive.
- **Lazy load.** The seed modules (~0.8 MB, ~23 ms to evaluate on the laptop) are imported only by `japaneseSeedLoader.ts`, and only when `japanese.seeded.version` differs from `SEED_VERSION` (the content hash, pinned by test). `index.ts` starts it after `initDatabase`; course/review/roadmap/stats channels and the deck builders that create courses (core, prep, grammar) await it via `seeded()` in `ipc.ts`. A failed seed logs and resolves so the section keeps working; the unwritten stamp retries next launch.
- **Steps come from pack order.** A course's `difficulty` (its step) is its index in `PACK_ORDER` + 1, stamped when `ALL_PACKS` is built; course files are `SeedCourseDef` and carry no step literal. Slotting a course in is one `PACK_ORDER` line: the sync pushes the new steps to unedited course rows, and `tests/japaneseSeed.test.ts` looks steps up from its `PATH` list instead of hardcoding them. The legacy `reorderSteps*` tables still read each pack's current step as their target.
- **Tutor phases** no longer read step numbers: `@shared/japanese/coursePhases.ts` maps each built-in course's seed key to a phase (user courses fall back to their JLPT level label, then to the original 26-step thresholds), so steps can be renumbered through the sync without moving a phase. `JpCourse.seedKey` carries the key to the renderer.

## Built-in content waves (2026-10-16)

The guided path grew from 27 to 32 steps and every grammar lesson now has at least five example cards. New course files live in `src/main/db/jpSeed/` (one per course, descriptive frozen lesson keys such as `n3c.toshitara`); the older `japaneseSeed*.ts` files stay where they are.

- **N5/N4.** Eighteen spine lessons (`jpSeed/n5Spine.ts`: が, は vs が, ましょう, たい, plain ない, から/ので, ている and the rest) are spliced into JLPT N5 Foundations at their teaching positions, and JLPT N4 Grammar II (`n4b`, step 8) covers the N4 points the first course missed. ものの (`n1a.01`) moved to N2 Grammar II through the sync's cross-course lesson move.
- **N3/N2/N1.** JLPT N3 Grammar III (`n3c`), N2 Grammar III and IV (`n2c` connectors and formal particle compounds, `n2d` clause patterns) and N1 Grammar III and IV (`n1c` noun-attached, `n1d` clause patterns) slot in after each level's Grammar II. The syllabus checklist was the hanabira grammar bank's point titles (used only as a list; explanations and sentences are original). Sub-senses of existing lessons (ところだった, によれば, だけしか, に限り, ではあるまいし, がままに, …) were folded into their host lesson as a paragraph and a card instead of becoming lessons. After the waves, the only hanabira N3/N2 titles without a matching lesson are spelling variants of covered points.
- **Examples.** Every N5–N3 vocabulary card has an example sentence (reading and English); the two JLPT vocabulary decks above them already did.
- **Deck tracks beside the Path** (`@shared/japanese/pathTracks.ts`). The curated courses teach about 600 words and 715 kanji; reading needs about 10,000 and 2,100. Two tracks fill the gap from the offline dictionaries: core word decks (`coreDeck.ts`) and kanji decks (`kanjiDeck.ts`: kanji no kanji card covers yet, the learner's own words' kanji first, then KANJIDIC frequency, with an example word from the learner's cards or the frequency list; jōyō or top-2,500 only; lessons of 20). Each track has frozen checkpoint targets keyed by course seed key, reached when that course is cleared; `roadmap()` reports `tracks.words`/`tracks.kanji` (cards built, target, next unlearned deck lesson) from the deck title patterns. The Path shows one row after each checkpoint course with the track's span, and the Build button and next lesson sit on the first unfinished checkpoint. Tracks never gate the courses. Today's tutor plan takes the next deck step (`nextDeckStep`: a built deck's next lesson, else a new deck when built < target; kanji every third day when both tracks have one) in the new-material slot on alternate local days, only when no unseen cards are waiting, so deck lessons are never auto-marked learned (that would hold every grammar lesson behind hundreds of unseen cards). The small JLPT vocabulary and kanji course descriptions now say they are starter sets and point at the tracks; titles are unchanged because the legacy reorder tables match by title.
- **Graded reading, second set.** `readings/{n4,n3,n2}more.ts` add 16 passages per level (25 each at N4–N2; 90 in all): stories, notices, emails, letters, interviews, reviews, news, instructions and opinion. Keys are frozen (`quiz_session.settings.passageKey` stores them). Each batch was checked with a kuromoji reading audit (bracket readings against the tokenizer, mismatches reviewed by hand) and an independent review of readings, level, answer keys and explanations; `tests/jpReadings.test.ts` now requires 25 passages at N4–N2.
- **Screen pass, Jisho/Satori style (2026-10-17).** Dictionary entries turn JMdict codes into readable labels from the dictionary's own `tag` table (`dict:tagInfo` → `@shared/japanese/dictTags.ts`): every frequency band (⭐, ichi, newsNk, spec, gai, nf) folds into one Common chip, bare sense-number tags are dropped, senses are numbered with the part of speech shown only when it changes, and the dictionary is named only when an entry mixes dictionaries. Each result shows its deck status (`japanese:cardsForFronts`, learned lessons first), and the side panel lists the results "In your decks". Graded reading makes every word a button (`@shared/japanese/readableUnits.ts` merges `analyzeText` tokens with the furigana notation and returns null on any mismatch, so the page falls back to plain furigana): words not yet started carry a dotted underline, a click opens a card with reading, first gloss, deck status, Listen, Save to review and Full entry, and each paragraph has its own Listen button; the side panel counts known words and lists up to 12 unknown ones. A course page with more than eight lessons groups them into units of six (closed once complete) under an Up next card with Continue, and the authoring buttons live in the More menu. The Grammar page has two tabs: Course lessons (every built-in grammar lesson in path order, `japanese:grammarIndex`, sharing the level filter and search) and the Reference pack. Mine shows the last cards saved to its target deck; Analyze shows its panel only after a run.
- **Content gates** (`tests/japaneseSeedContent.test.ts`): every card reading and example reading matches kuromoji's, or is a hand-reviewed entry in `tests/fixtures/jpSeedReadingExceptions.json` (kuromoji misreads 一人, 二人, 日本, 何, 者 and similar; a stale entry fails too); a vocabulary example contains its word or stem; no stray spaces in a Japanese front or reading; grammar lessons explain themselves and have at least five cards; no front repeats in a lesson; no emoji or decorative glyphs. Each batch was also checked by an independent review pass for grammar, readings, translation emphasis and ungendered English.

## Furigana (2026-10-16)

Readings above kanji come from the content's own stored reading, never from a font or a guessed per-kanji reading. `alignFurigana` (`@shared/japanese/furigana.ts`) gives each kanji run a slice of the reading anchored on the kana between runs, and uses the split only when it is the only one possible; a kana anchor that also occurs inside a reading (母は料理 → はは|は or は|はは…) leaves several, so the caller shows the reading whole instead. About 1.5% of seed sentences take that fallback; before this check the shortest split was used and roughly half of those showed a wrong reading. `ReadingText` (`components/japanese/Furigana.tsx`) renders either form. The lesson page shows furigana on grammar sentences, kanji example words and vocabulary example sentences (now listed under each meaning), behind one Furigana toggle that the graded-reading page shares (`japanese.furigana` in localStorage, `lib/japanesePrefs.ts`).

## FSRS scheduler (2026-10-16)

`@shared/srs.ts` is now FSRS-5 (default parameters) behind the same exported names (`gradeCard`, `previewIntervals`, `newCardState`, the leech/ghost constants), shared by the Japanese and English decks. Each card keeps a memory state, `stability` (days until recall falls to 90%) and `fsrs_difficulty` (1–10), on `jp_card`/`en_word`; `ease` is still written but no longer read. The 1 m / 10 m learning and 10 m relearning steps stay (FSRS's short-term update covers same-day answers); graduation, Hard/Good/Easy and lapses take their intervals from the stability at the desired retention, with Hard ≤ Good < Easy enforced and the ±5% cohort fuzz kept (never in previews). Intervals cap at 3,650 days.

- **Inputs.** `submitReview` (both repos) passes the whole local calendar days since `last_reviewed_at` (`calendarDaysSince`), not the overdue gap: FSRS credits a long survival and discounts an early answer by itself, its weights were fitted on whole days, and a same-calendar-day answer (a learning step, even one left until late evening) takes the short-term update. A lapse is capped at S / e^(w17·w18) (the FSRS-5 rule), so lapse-then-relearn never ends more stable than before. Desired retention is the `srs.desiredRetention` settings row (Settings → Learning → Review scheduler: 85/90/95%, default 90%), read in main by `srsSettings.desiredRetention()` and in the review pages by `lib/srsPreview.useRetention()`, so the button previews equal what is written. `submitReview` returns the persisted `SrsState`, and a requeued learning step continues from it (gap 0) rather than a renderer-side recomputation.
- **Logs.** `jp_review_log`/`en_review_log` gained `state_before` (`new`/`learning`/`relearning`/`review`), `elapsed_days` (calendar days, NULL for a first answer) and `scheduled_days` (the review interval the card had, 0 in the step ladder) — what an FSRS optimizer reads — via `reviewLogValues`, so per-user fitting can come later without guessing history. Pre-FSRS rows keep NULLs.
- **Migration** (`db/fsrsMigration.ts`, once per deck, flags `srs.fsrs.jp`/`srs.fsrs.en`, from `runMigrations`). Every card with `reps > 0` and no stability replays its own last `reps` log rows (a reset keeps old rows) through `nextMemory`. A card with state but no usable log (an "Already known" word) gets stability = its interval and a difficulty read off its ease. Status, due date and interval are left alone, so nothing jumps in the queue; the next answer schedules from the derived state. `resetCard` clears the memory state and `last_reviewed_at`; `markWordsKnown` writes stability = its 36,500-day interval and difficulty 1.
- **Leeches** order by difficulty instead of ease; the stats page shows it.

## Review directions (2026-10-16)

Besides recognition (the card itself, Japanese → meaning), vocabulary earns two more schedules in `jp_card_review` (`@shared/japanese/reviewDirections.ts`): **production** (meaning → word, typed in kanji, kana or romaji — typing the reading counts) and **reading** (a kanji word → its reading; needs kanji and a reading that differs). Grammar and kanji lessons get neither: grammar's practice is its cloze, kanji readings are drilled on the kana page.

- **Recognition stays on `jp_card`.** Every "is this word known" read — coverage, the i+1 feed fingerprint, the JLPT ladder, quiz pools, leeches — is unchanged. A direction row carries only its own FSRS schedule; `mapDirectionCard` overlays it on the card's content, and `JpReviewCard.direction` tells the page which schedule it answers.
- **Spawning.** Only inside `submitReview`, whenever a recognition answer leaves the card in review at `DIRECTION_SPAWN_INTERVAL_DAYS` (3) or more, never in a read path. Rows enter as `new`. Spawning pauses while `DIRECTION_BACKLOG_CAP` (30) new direction rows wait — each word can unlock two while the queue serves them only part of the budget, so the backlog would otherwise grow forever — and because it retries on every later mature review, words that matured before directions existed or while one was off get theirs at their next review. Words in the "Already known" lesson never spawn. `updateCard` drops a direction an edit made impossible (reading cleared, kana-only front); `resetCard` drops the rows (they respawn on graduation); deleting a card cascades.
- **Queue and budget.** Due directions merge into the due list by due time. New directions share the session's new-card budget, taking at most half of it (floor; more only when there are not enough new words). **Sibling burying:** a word is dealt under one schedule per session (recognition first, then its first direction), so a recognition reveal never hands over the next prompt's answer; ghosts skip words already in the session. `stats().dueCount`, the roadmap's `due_count` and `dueForecast` include directions. `newAvailableCount` counts new **words** only — the Tutor and Home hold the next lesson on it, and an unlocked direction of a known word must not — while `newDirectionCount` is separate; the review page's session size, Home's review load and the learning band use their sum. `introducedToday` groups by `(card_id, direction)`, so each direction is its own introduction (and the `jpReviews` checklist counts distinct card+direction). All direction reads share `directionScope()`.
- **Logging.** `jp_review_log.direction` (`recognition` default). Retention (strict/lenient, all-time and 30-day, which the Tutor plans from) reads recognition answers only, because typed production is harder by nature; the heatmap, streak and grade counts include every answer. `listLeeches` counts recognition answers only (its `reps` window is the card's); ghosts echo recognition lapses only.
- **Settings.** Settings → Learning → "Japanese review directions" toggles each (`japanese.direction.production` / `.reading`, missing = on). Off stops spawning and hides the direction's reviews from the queue and counts; its rows and progress stay.
- **Review page.** Directions are always typed through `buildDirectionPrompt` (with Reveal instead for self-grading) and show a direction chip; grammar sentences now default to their cloze whatever the typed toggle says; other recognition cards follow the toggle.

## Tokenizer: backend seam, casual-speech layer, measurement (2026-10-16)

`src/main/tokenizer.ts` now puts kuromoji (IPAdic) behind a `TokenizerBackend` interface (`kuromojiBackend`, `setTokenizerBackend` for tests and evaluation); the eight callers still call `tokenize()`. A backend returns IPAdic-shaped tokens, because `sentenceGames`/`chunkTokens` and the drills read IPAdic POS names — a UniDic backend would need a POS mapping (補助記号, 形状詞, …).

**Casual-speech layer** (`tokenizerCasual.ts`). IPAdic knows the standard contractions (てる, ちゃう, じゃん, っす, なきゃ, とく) but splits sound-changed slang into nonsense (す/げ/え, や/べ/ー). `normalizeCasualText` rewrites each colloquial form into its standard spelling **of the same length** — generated from a curated adjective list (a-/o-row + い ↔ e-row + え/ぇ/ー, u-row + い ↔ i-row + い/ぃ/ー, わい ↔ ええ, らない ↔ んねえ) plus the verb negative ねえ → ない only after an a-row stem or ん (so "ねえ、" and 姉さん are untouched) — tokenizes that, and `restoreSurfaces` gives every token the original characters at the same offsets: dictionary forms and POS come from the standard text, the surface and kana reading stay as written. Forms that collide with other words (おめえ = お前, くれえ) are deliberately not generated. Any coverage mismatch falls back to tokenizing the original.

**Measurement** (`tests/fixtures/jpTokenizerCorpus.json`, 175 manga/VN lines with the content words a learner would mine, 376 words; `tests/tokenizerCorpus.test.ts` pins the casual layer above raw kuromoji and above 90%). On 2026-10-16:

| Tokenizer | Words found | Slang | Contractions | Garbage single-kana nouns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kuromoji (IPAdic) | 88.3% | 19/36 | 57/64 | 13 |
| kuromoji + casual layer (shipped) | 91.2% | 30/36 | 57/64 | 8 |
| lindera UniDic (`lindera-wasm-nodejs-unidic` 2.0.0, 45 MB) | 92.3% | 27/36 | 63/64 | 0 |
| lindera UniDic + casual layer | 92.8% | 29/36 | 63/64 | 0 |

UniDic is cleaner (no garbage tokens, better on contractions and questions, slang handled natively) at 45 MB instead of kuromoji's ~18 MB, a ~1.3 s dictionary build, and a POS-scheme migration for the sentence games. The comparison ran from a scratch install; swapping is a separate decision.

## Offline voice: Open JTalk in WebAssembly (2026-10-16)

Seeded and mined words had no audio except mined clips, Tatoeba sentences and minimal pairs. Open JTalk 1.11 + hts_engine_API 1.10 are now compiled to WebAssembly by `scripts/build-openjtalk-wasm.sh` (pinned Emscripten 3.1.74; sources are the Ubuntu archive's copies of the upstream tarballs, checked against their `.dsc` SHA-256s; one patch, `HTS_ftell` → `ftell`; MeCab compiled as C++11) and committed as `resources/openjtalk/open_jtalk.{js,wasm}` (~450 KB, Node target, `callMain`), shipped via `extraResources`. Provenance and licences: `resources/openjtalk/README.md`.

- **Voice pack** (`tts/openJTalkPack.ts`): the NAIST-jdic dictionary for Open JTalk (23.6 MB, 107 MB unpacked) and the NIT ATR503 M001 voice (0.8 MB, CC BY 3.0, credited in Settings), downloaded once at setup into `<userData>/openjtalk/{dic,voice}`. Each archive goes through the dictionary import gate (`runImport`, a task row titled "Japanese voice", cancel reaching the extraction too), downloads under its own size cap (`downloadToTemp`'s `maxBytes`), is checked against its pinned SHA-256 **before** extraction, is extracted by a streaming tar reader (`tts/tarExtract.ts`) that writes only whitelisted names under the archive's own root (no `..`, no absolute paths) with an unpacked-size cap and fails cleanly on any output error, and is swapped in from a staging folder only when every expected file is present — the old folder is moved aside first and put back if the final rename fails. It is the seventh starter pack (Today's setup list, `starterPacks.ts`) and a row in Settings → Learning → Japanese dictionaries.
- **Synthesis** runs in a worker thread (`tts/ttsWorker.ts`, bundled via `?modulePath`, no electron import), so a ~150 ms synthesis never blocks main; one engine instance serves requests in order. Each run reloads the dictionary through the CLI's `main()` (there is no persistent API in the stock engine); measured 120–210 ms per phrase. The worker stops itself after three quiet minutes (an unref'd one-shot timeout, not an interval) so its ~100 MB engine heap is not held all session; worker events settle only their own worker's jobs, so a replaced worker's late exit cannot fail the new one; install and remove hold synthesis off while the pack folders are swapped.
- **Reading hold**: `SpeakButton` passes the reading the screen shows. The worker speaks the written form first (it carries the pitch accent: 橋 vs 箸) and reads the engine's chosen reading from its trace (`-ot`, `readingFromTrace`); if it differs (今日 read きょう on a こんにち card, 上手 じょうず on an うわて card) the reading itself is spoken instead. The reading is part of the cache key. `tts/ttsRuntime.ts` owns the worker and is reached only through `import()` from `ipc.ts` (guarded in `performanceBoundaries`); `ttsPaths.stopActiveTts()` is in the before-quit registry. The voice runs at its native 48 kHz — lowering `-s` alone keeps the 48 kHz frame period and plays at half speed — and `TTS_VOICE_ID` is part of every cache key so a format change never replays an old clip.
- **Cache**: `ttscache/<sha1(voice|text|reading)>.wav` (`files.ttsCacheDir`, its own navimg prefix), written under `.partial` and renamed, and only when the output is a real RIFF/WAVE clip; concurrent requests for one text share a synthesis; pruned oldest-first past 300 MB (checked every 50 writes). Deliberately not under `jpaudio/`, which library backups carry. Removing the voice removes the pack and the cache.
- **Playback**: `components/japanese/SpeakButton.tsx` renders only when the pack is installed and plays through a page-local `Audio` (never the global player). It sits on review reveals (the word, or a kanji card's example word, and the example sentence), lesson card tables and sentence cards, dictionary headwords, and dictionary examples that have no Tatoeba recording (a native recording always wins). The tooltip says it is a synthetic voice.
- **Accent**: Open JTalk takes pitch accent from its own dictionary (橋 2, 箸 1, 端 0 verified), which can differ from Kanjium's graphs; for a word with several readings it picks one, which is why kanji cards speak their example word instead of the lone character.

## Saved VN text captures

`/visual-novels/:id/study` saves paste input or UTF-8 text logs in `vn_text_capture`
(maximum 200,000 characters; file input also caps at 800 KB). Captures retain their
VN, date and optional reading entry. Normalized text fingerprints reject duplicate
captures per VN. Raw text stays readable without study resources. Analysis reuses
the offline tokenizer, shared `AnalysisView`, dictionary/mining panel and source-aware
review-card creation. Word selection retains its paragraph and VN; analysis is an
explicit snapshot and can be rerun after card changes.

`seriesText.seriesCorpus` selects saved captures for VNs. Coverage and preparation
decks therefore use the existing study pipeline. Synchronous tokenizer chunks are
bounded at 4,000 characters. Captured character counts and comprehension describe
only the saved sample, not the entire game or demonstrated mastery. Coverage uses
review cards plus the configured frequency baseline, which the UI identifies.

Editing or deleting a capture invalidates `jp_coverage` and `jp_coverage_word` in the
same transaction. Fingerprints are checked after tokenization and again before a
preparation deck writes, preventing a concurrent edit from restoring stale coverage.
Existing mined cards survive deletion. Captures are personal and wiped on export.
No live game hook, remote study service or OCR is needed for this workflow.


## Dictionary import resource boundaries

Remote Yomitan archives stream to unique temporary files and are deleted after success or failure.
An archive may be at most 2 GiB, and no decompressed entry may exceed 256 MiB; both declared and
observed entry sizes are checked before a bank is buffered. Tatoeba audio clips use a separate
16 MiB per-file ceiling. These are import-time safety limits only: installed dictionary lookup and
study remain fully offline.

## The JLPT ladder

**Levels as progress, never a gate (2026-08-16).** `@shared/jlptLevels.ts` owns the rules; `japaneseRepo.jlptLadder()` is one pass over `jp_card ⋈ jp_lesson ⋈ jp_course`; `components/japanese/JlptLadder.tsx` renders it in a "JLPT ladder" section on the roadmap page.

**No schema change.** Courses already carry a free-text level LABEL (`'N5'`, `'N5+'`, `'N4–N3'`, `'N3–N2'` — what the seeds write and the course pages show). `parseJlptLevel` takes the FIRST tier a label names: the tier the course is pitched at, with the range's upper end treated as stretch material. Higher-numbered N is easier, so "first mentioned" is also "lowest". A label naming no tier yields null, and those cards are reported as `unlevelled` rather than dropped — a total the user cannot reconcile with their library is worse than a caveat.

**"Passed" is defined in this app's terms.** The SRS here is Anki-shaped (`new`/`learning`/`review`/`relearn` + `interval_days`), not WaniKani's stage ladder, so Guru has no direct equivalent. A card counts once it is `status='review'` with `interval_days >= PASSED_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7) — it has left the learning steps and earned a real interval. The UI states that definition in its footnote instead of borrowing WaniKani's word without its machinery.

A level clears at `LEVEL_PASS_RATIO` (90%), the user's choice, so a few stubborn cards cannot stall it. `currentLevel` is the easiest uncleared level, **skipping levels with no cards** — a library that starts at N3 must not read as "you are on N5 forever" — and settles on the hardest level with cards once everything is cleared.

**Nothing locks.** The user decided levels track progress and gate nothing: this section is their only Japanese source, so it must never be the reason a card is unavailable. `tests/jlptLadder.test.ts` pins both the pure rules and the real SQL.

Pure parts: `@shared/kanaKeyboard.ts` (layout tables + dakuten/handakuten/small toggles + the phone ゛゜小 `cycleKana` orbit base→small→dakuten→handakuten) and `@shared/imeCompose.ts` (`ComposeState {kana, pending}` — typing.ts's interpret() promoted; re-converts the whole pending tail per keystroke, holds a lone trailing "n"; no-op returns the SAME object). **There is no katakana mode** — the engine composes hiragana and the candidate row leads with the two script chips (hira + `toKatakana`), which is also the empty-dict degradation. Candidates: `dict:readingCandidates` (`dict/imeCandidates.ts`) — exact reading match (both scripts, `d.priority >= 0` excludes JMnedict, dedupe by expression, rank freq→common-tag→dict-priority→score, script-form expressions EXCLUDED as chip duplicates) then freq-GATED LIKE-prefix predictions (≥2 kana only), cap 16, `[]` on any failure; renderer queries it via `qk.dict.imeCandidates` on a 150ms-debounced `commitText`. Load-bearing renderer details: EVERY panel control `preventDefault`s pointerdown (focus must never leave the host input), caret restored via rAF `setSelectionRange` after controlled onChange, physical typing bypasses the buffer entirely (goes straight to the input) EXCEPT Enter/Escape, which route through the `JpKeyboardBridge` ref first — Enter with uncommitted kana COMMITS it (never submits the stale value), Escape clears the buffer, then closes the panel, then reaches page cascades (`stopPropagation`). Prefs (layout + romaji hints via `kanaToRomaji` sublabels) in localStorage `jp.keyboardPrefs` (book.readerPrefs idiom). This wave also extended romaji.ts BASE/DIGRAPHS (x/l small kana, ゔ/`vu`+combos, ファ/ティ/ウィ/シェ families) — app-wide effect on `romajiToHiragana`/`acceptedRomaji`, safe because matchState's documented failure direction is "too lenient". Tests: kanaKeyboard (completeness/orbits), imeCompose (keystroke-vs-full-string equivalence), dictIme (real dict schema). GUI smoke pending on the user's machine (headless box).


## Wave 4 — sentence games, arcade, an honest JLPT test, graded reading

**Japanese wave 4 (2026-08-15)** — three additions that generate their own content, one that stops lying, and two new seeded courses. **Foundations**: `JpToken.posDetail` (kuromoji `pos_detail_1`, `null` for `*` — the one construction site is `tokenizer.ts`); QuizKind += `particles scramble contextReading kanaRace readingRace conjRace jpReading`, all added to `JP_QUIZ_KINDS` (now exported and guarded by NEW `tests/jpQuizKindsSync.test.ts`, which reads the kind literals under `pages/Japanese*` + `components/japanese/` as TEXT and diffs them against the list, filtered through the QuizKind union so a lesson `kind: 'cards'` never trips it); `quizRepo.SCORE_RANKED_KINDS` (the three races) makes `history()` rank the record by **score** rather than accuracy — a time attack's record is most-correct-in-the-minute, and ranking by ratio would reward slow careful play (Shiritori's score=total hack predates this); `api.quiz.history(kind, limit?)` + `qk.quiz.historyAll(kind)` (under the `history(kind)` prefix, so one invalidation refreshes both) exist for the per-passage bests below. `@shared/kanaRows.ts` and `@shared/dojoWords.ts` lift the kana row tables and the conjugation dojo words out of `JapaneseKanaPage` verbatim so the arcade can share them; `dojoWords.ts` adds pure `posToWordClass(pos, front)` mapping a seeded card's `pos` onto a conjugation class.

**Sentence games** (`/japanese/sentences`, tabs particles | scramble | reading, `?tab=` seeded): pure model in NEW `@shared/japanese/sentenceGames.ts` — `eligibleParticles` (whitelisted 助詞, never the sentence-final one, never adjacent to another particle so には/では stay whole), `blankAt`, `particleOptions` with **`PARTICLE_CONFLICTS`**: a distractor that would often ALSO be correct (が for は, も for either, へ for に) is never offered, which is the honest answer to は/が — exclusion, not accept-both, which would hand over a free elimination; `chunkTokens` (bunsetsu rule: 接頭詞 binds forward, 助詞/助動詞/記号 and posDetail 接尾/非自立 attach backwards, 名詞+名詞 merges unless the first is 代名詞); `stripFinalPunct`; `contextCandidates` (kanji-bearing, reading present, 名詞 minus 数/接尾/非自立 or a dictionary-form 動詞/形容詞 — so the JMdict check is exact). Pools in NEW `src/main/jpSentenceGames.ts` over NEW `dict/sentences.ts:sampleSentences({minChars,maxChars,limit})`; `contextReadingPool` accepts a word ONLY when kuromoji's reading is among JMdict's for that expression (`d.priority >= 0`, direct SQL) and returns every attested reading so 魚 takes さかな and うお alike. Channels `japanese:particlePool | scramblePool | contextReadingPool`; renderer `JapaneseSentencesPage` + `ParticleDrill` (McDrill, 4-up options, audio when the pack has the clip) / `ScrambleDrill` (own loop — McDrill is single-pick: tray → answer row, keys 1-N, Backspace undoes, Enter checks; copy is explicit that it grades against the ORIGINAL order and yours may still be grammatical) / `ContextReadingDrill` (TypedDrill with a `node` prompt highlighting the target).
