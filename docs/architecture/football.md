## Product and identity

Football is a standalone section, not a `MediaType`. Historical seasons and matches would overwhelm the ordinary media library, and their tracking model is different: favorites exist across competitions, teams, people, and matches, while only matches have watched time, a 0-5 half-star rating, and a private note.

The frozen competition keys are `premier-league`, `la-liga`, `serie-a`, `bundesliga`, `champions-league`, `europa-league`, `conference-league`, `world-cup`, and `euros`. The archive preserves the First Division, European Cup, and UEFA Cup lineages. It excludes the Fairs Cup and international qualification.

`football_source_ref` owns provider identities. A source id is always scoped by source; a normalized label alone never merges people. Exact team aliases may resolve when there is one canonical candidate. A person joins an existing same-name person only when exactly one such person appeared for the same team within fifteen years of the match (or scored for that team in the same match under a source being replaced); a scorer's source key is season-scoped, but the identity is team-scoped, so a player who scores across seasons stays one person. Any other same-name person makes the new row an ambiguous identity: it creates a `football_conflict` row and stays out of quizzes until resolved. `repairPersonIdentities()` runs after history installs and overlay packs, and from the resolution queue's bulk button: it merges same-team splits and a player whose citizenship matches the national team exactly one namesake played for (skipping people the user kept separate or ignored, and never joining namesakes with different birth dates, or one who played before the other turned fifteen), deletes people nothing refers to any more (typically a scorer whose match goals another source replaced; favourites, media, lists, careers, pictures and quiz-pack flags all count as references), and closes identity conflicts with no remaining same-name person or only provably different namesakes. Identity lookups filter the event and lineup tables by person before joining, and import loops reuse prepared statements through `cachedStatement`: an appearance union evaluated per new player, and a fresh prepared statement per call, together ran the Transfermarkt import out of memory once. The queue lists each identity conflict's same-name candidates with their teams and years, so a merge never needs a typed id. Cross-source result disagreements also create a conflict instead of overwriting the canonical score. The resolution queue can accept either stored assertion, merge a team/person into an explicit target, confirm that identities are separate, or ignore a row. Ignored conflicts remain quarantined and continue to exclude their facts from quizzes.

The 24-table vertical is split into:

- archive graph: competition, era, season, stage, team, person, tenure, match, lineup, event, standing, and honour;
- source integrity: alias, source ref, assertion, coverage, conflict, import run, and article;
- personal layer: favorite, match journal, media, media link, and external link.

Every coverage facet says `complete`, `partial`, `conflicted`, or `not_supplied`. Missing scorer or lineup children never mean an empty factual set. Coverage is source-scoped in the UI; different providers for one facet are not collapsed into one optimistic label. Competition-level coverage has a partial unique index for `(competition, source, facet)` because SQLite's ordinary composite uniqueness does not constrain repeated `NULL` season ids; startup deduplicates legacy rows before installing that index. Domestic history gets a calculated W-D-L/goals/points ledger using the season's points-for-a-win rule, but its ordinal rank remains null unless an official provider supplies a tested rank that includes deductions and tie-breaks.

## Source stack and sync

The first install avoids Wrestling's page-by-page crawl:

1. `engsoccerdata` contributes heterogeneous bulk CSV histories for England, Spain, Italy, Germany, plus European Cup/Champions League results through its archive. England and Germany are filtered to `tier=1` (germany.csv also carries the 2. Bundesliga; Spain and Italy are tier 1 only). Archives installed before the Germany filter report the History step as pending whenever a Bundesliga season holds more than 380 matches, so Continue setup re-runs it and the complete-refresh prune removes the tier-2 rows; History then deletes clubs nothing refers to any more (`removeOrphanTeams`). Every write loop passes through `checkpoint()`, which yields a macrotask so status polls, pause and cancel get through between slices, and the write paths reuse prepared statements through `cachedStatement`. Adapters validate the real header of every file.
2. OpenFootball's CC0 tournament repository complements UEFA history. Its JSON and Football.TXT forms have explicit adapters for variable full-time, extra-time, penalty, scorer, own-goal, and stoppage-time representations. A missing scorer line stays `not_supplied`, never zero goals.
3. `international_results` cross-checks World Cup and Euros finals-tournament results and goal scorers. Scorers join only when date/home/away identifies exactly one match.
4. A frozen manifest of exactly nine English Wikipedia pages supplies explicit edition winners, runners-up, competition narrative, revision, license, and attribution. Each entry also freezes its first edition and a conservative row floor; a refresh must satisfy both and cannot shrink the last complete edition set before it may prune. The importer reads source wikitext and stores plain text; remote HTML is never rendered. A winner is never inferred from a calculated table.
5. API-Football supplies manually refreshed current fixtures, status, official tables, top-20 scorers, and changed fixture goal/lineup detail when the configured plan exposes them. The key is sent only as `x-apisports-key`. Its keyless media host (`media.api-sports.io`) supplies a club crest only when Wikipedia and TheSportsDB have none.

StatsBomb and Wyscout are reduced optional overlay sources on the Sync page. Both require one explicitly selected competition, share the `footballSync` singleton, and are never part of initial installation. StatsBomb reads its official open-data season manifest and then fetches that season's match, event, and lineup JSON. A blank season selects the newest supported men's edition; an explicit season must match the manifest. Wyscout uses the fixed CC BY 4.0 Figshare release: the four domestic leagues are 2017/18, World Cup is 2018, and Euros is 2016. Its event archive is about 74 MB, is bounded at 96 MiB, and the UI warns before the user starts it. Unsupported competition-season requests fail before any canonical writes.

Each completed overlay match writes verified goal and lineup detail. A detail refresh replaces goals and lineups independently: an omitted or incomplete facet preserves the last complete children instead of treating absence as an empty list. StatsBomb and Wyscout people retain source-scoped ids; a matching normalized name creates a resolution conflict rather than an automatic person merge. Coverage becomes complete only after every match in the selected pack has committed that facet, with supplied counts recorded for partial packs. Cancellation or a failed detail request therefore leaves the last honest coverage state and every previously completed match usable.

`src/main/football/sync.ts` is the one `footballSync` singleton. History, current refresh, optional packs, and lazy entity enrichment cannot overlap. It exposes a polled status and a task-registry projection with cooperative pause/resume/cancel; there is no timer, push channel, or automatic refresh. Cancellation occurs between authoritative slices and on active fetches, so committed slices remain usable.

API-Football's daily budget is persisted in `football.api_quota`. Entitlements are checked per competition-season. The refresh spends requests on the league check and fixtures first, then supported standings and top scorers, then unfinished goal/lineup details. Work that cannot fit stays visible as backlog; the next manual refresh sees incomplete fixture coverage and resumes it.

Wikimedia/Wikidata enrichment is lazy. Opening an entity never starts a network request; Save and the explicit reference button can queue it. Operational fetch failures set a retryable enrichment error and do not manufacture identity conflicts. The article record stores plain text, source URL, revision, license, attribution, and state. Identity is resolved from the name's likely article titles (`X F.C.`, `FC X`, `X national football team`, `X (footballer)`), falling back to a title search; a candidate must match the name's distinctive words (club suffixes stripped) and be confirmed on Wikidata as association football (`P641` = Q2736, or a footballer/manager occupation), or the fetch is ambiguous. Images are the infobox image whatever its license (the user's 2026-09-28 directive: the section must look official), downloaded as a PNG raster so SVG crests work; Wikidata `P154`, TheSportsDB badges and API-Football media are fallbacks in that order. Wikidata also supplies club colours (`P465` on the club, else `P6364` colour items), stadium and capacity (`P115`, `P1083`), and player position, height, birthplace and sporting nationality (`P413`, `P2048`, `P19`, `P1532`). An ordinary fetch stores resolved senior-club spells for the career views, marked incomplete unless they meet the quiz rule. Sources → Fetch pictures and facts runs the batch `artwork` sync kind: nine competition logos, clubs by match count, then players with a footprint in the archive; completed entities are skipped so a paused run resumes. TheSportsDB calls are spaced over two seconds apart for its free tier. Wikipedia, Wikidata and Commons are paced by the process-wide `wikimediaThrottle` inside `fetchWithRetry` (Wikimedia's 200 requests a minute cover all three and every feature that calls them), which also sets `WIKIMEDIA_USER_AGENT`; an image already on disk takes no slot. Thumbnails use the standard 330 and 500 px steps, stored without the API's `utm_` query (`stripWikimediaTracking`) so file names stay stable. A competition whose article has no infobox image (the FIFA World Cup) takes its Wikidata logo (`P154`), the preferred-rank statement when there is one and never a deprecated one. Crest and logo slots load the cached thumbnail (`StoredMark`), which keeps transparency. In the renderer, `useFootballCompetitionLogos()` reads every stored logo once through the light `football:competitionLogos` channel (never `listCompetitions`, which sums goals over every match), so `FootballFlag` chips and `FootballCompetitionMark` show the logo wherever only a key is at hand; logos sit on a white tile because several are dark artwork on a transparent background, and team crests carry the `.football-crest` halo in the text colour so dark crests stay visible. Honours carry `competitionKey` for their logo, and the champion, scoreline and career-path quizzes carry team crests (`imagePath` on choices and spells) without changing how a round is dealt. The optional Player Quiz Pack deterministically selects up to 250 connected players, resolves a unique Wikimedia identity, and stores structured Wikidata senior-club memberships. Refreshing the pack clears stale career rows first, and a career enters the quiz pool only when it has at least four dated, verified, complete spells and no unresolved or ignored identity conflict.

### Setup checklist

`/football/sync` leads with three ordered setup steps: match history, match detail (Transfermarkt) and pictures and facts. The `setup` sync kind runs the unfinished steps in order as one pausable task (`setupSteps` narrows it; with every step done it refreshes all three), and each step records its completion in the `football.setup.history|detail|pictures` settings rows, read back as `FootballSetupState` on the sync overview and on Football Home. A history install from before these rows existed is shown as installed with a refresh recommended. Home shows a setup strip until all three have finished once. The resolution queue appears only when something is open; API-Football's current season is one optional line; StatsBomb, Wyscout, the Player Quiz Pack, the coverage matrix and recent runs sit under a collapsed "Extra sources and details".

### Transfermarkt match detail

Sources → Install match detail (`transfermarkt` sync kind, `src/main/football/transfermarkt.ts`) streams the public CC0 transfermarkt-datasets release (about 230 MB of gzipped CSV, 2012 onward) through a temporary directory that is always removed. It never creates matches: `linkTmGames` learns each Transfermarkt club's archive team from games that agree on competition, date (plus or minus one day) and score, with a bonus for a matching distinctive name, then links a game to the one archive match between the two mapped teams within two days. Linked games write referee, attendance and venue only where the archive has none, formations and managers, full lineups when both sides list eleven starters, goals with assists only when the per-team goal count equals the archive score (otherwise the archive's scorers stay), and cards, substitutions and shoot-out kicks as `card`, `substitution` and `shootout` events (a release without them for a game leaves the stored ones). Players resolve by Transfermarkt id, else one same-team same-name person, else a new person (quarantined when the name exists elsewhere); their birth date, position, foot, height and citizenship fill empty fields. Transfers up to today are stored in `football_transfer` for archive players, with club ids where the club is mapped. Coverage rows record linked versus available games per season.

## Current desk and media

Matchday (`/football/current`) is keyless by default: the `fixtures` sync kind (also run at the end of every history step) downloads OpenFootball's football.json files for this and last season of the four domestic leagues (`currentClubSeasonStart`: a season runs from July), about twice-weekly updated. `writeLeagueFixtures` first maps each file's club names ("Arsenal FC") onto the league's clubs from the last five seasons by distinctive name, then writes through `writeSlice` as a partial current slice (the previous season as complete). Source ids omit the date, so a rescheduled fixture updates in place. `football.fixtures.updated` and `football.fixtures.latestResult` feed the page's freshness line. The page shows one week at a time grouped by day, the table of the selected (or Premier League) season beside it, and API-Football's same-day refresh as an optional extra; FotMob remains the user's live-score source.


Scores are always visible. `/football/current` reads the last stored snapshot and never live-polls scores. Entitlement, facet coverage, refresh timestamp, quota, and backlog sit beside the data. When verified events are absent the UI says `Scorer data not supplied`.

Football media is manual only:

- local files must already be beneath `football.dir`; the DB stores a relative path, and `football/` resolves through the guarded `absoluteMediaPath` branch;
- HTTP(S) links open in the browser and are never embedded or downloaded;
- an attachment can link to many entities;
- footage linked to a match derives onto both teams and its competition without duplicate media rows;
- removing an attachment deletes only its DB row, never the underlying file.

FotMob is deliberately a validated external link provider, not a data source. Only pasted `fotmob.com` match, team, player, or league paths are stored and opened in the browser. No FotMob page or unsupported endpoint is scraped, cached, or embedded.

Competition, team, person, and match detail pages expose the same external-link editor, including ordinary website references. The directory and media shelves page through fixed-size IPC reads rather than imposing a hidden top-300 ceiling, and Football routes render explicit query failures instead of presenting them as valid empty archives.

Ordinary and ranked Lists accept `footballCompetition`, `footballTeam`, `footballPerson`, and `footballMatch`. Football kinds are absent from the Tier List form and filters in v1.

## Quiz pack

All five games use the shared `quiz:challengePool` contract and record solo metadata, seed, filters, score policy, and dataset revision:

- `footballChampion`: verified, non-shared, completed edition winners with era-near distractors;
- `footballScoreline`: finished, semantically complete matches; awarded, abandoned, aggregate-only, and conflicted rows are excluded;
- `footballCareerPath`: four to six displayed verified senior spells, loan labels, and an ellipsis when a longer trail is shortened;
- `footballChronology`: five four-edition boards, scored as twenty direct positions;
- `footballPlayerGrid`: a 3x3 player board with at least two answers per cell, three clue families, and a proven nine-player matching. It scores 100 unaided, 40 after a hint, and subtracts 10 only for a known invalid player.

Player Grid is points-ranked. The other four use the shared accuracy comparison and its five-answer minimum. Every builder is seeded and pure; partial, ambiguous, conflicted, or unsupported facts are filtered in the repository before a deal.

## Export posture

Shareable exports always wipe every `football_*` table, Football list items, `football.dir`, the API key/quota, cached paths, URLs, articles, images referenced by the catalog, and the personal journal. This is intentionally stricter than Wrestling because the combined Football catalog includes personal-use and provider-restricted snapshots.
