## The pipeline

`coreDeck.ts` (whole-language frequency deck, default 500 words, one course dump) and `prepDeck.ts` (per-series frequency deck, default 100 words, `WORDS_PER_LESSON=25` at prepDeck.ts:17) share `writeWordCourse` (prepDeck.ts:144-177) and both write UNLEARNED lessons — intentional, per the coverageRepo.ts:25-27 comment, so a deck dump doesn't instantly inflate comprehension. `seriesText.ts` unifies manga OCR blocks and video subtitle dialogue into one `CorpusUnit` stream (`seriesCorpus`, lines 71-85; `pickCorpusTrack` down-weights signs/forced tracks at lines 120-134) so the SAME scan powers both the prep deck and the comprehension scan (`coverage.ts`) with one definition of "a word" (`isLearnableWord`, seriesText.ts:35-40). `analyzeText.ts` is the same tiering machinery applied to arbitrary pasted text (a tweet, a linked article) rather than a scanned series.

The seeded curriculum (`japaneseSeed.ts`/`japaneseSeed2.ts`/`japaneseSeed3.ts`) is real: 24 courses, difficulty-tagged 1 through 24 with no gaps (verified: `grep difficulty: japaneseSeed*.ts` → {1,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,12,13,14,16,17,21,22} ∪ {2,5,9,15,20} ∪ {18,19,23,24} = exactly 1..24), roughly 2,130 authored cards (`grep -c front: japaneseSeed*.ts` → 1287+313+530), spanning N5→N1 grammar/vocab/kanji plus specialty courses (`Manga & VN Japanese` I/II at difficulty 4 and 10, `Kanji Radicals & Components`, `Counters & Numbers`, `Manga SFX & Onomatopoeia`, `Speech Styles & Role Language`, `Idioms & Set Phrases`). `japaneseSeed.ts:725-763` ("Manga & VN Japanese") is directly aimed at the user's stated goal: contractions (てる/でる, ちゃう/じゃう), sentence-final particles, gendered speech — content textbooks skip and manga is full of.

`JapaneseRoadmapPage.tsx` genuinely threads these into one path: a "you are here" frontier (`japaneseRepo.roadmap`, japaneseRepo.ts:505-559, first difficulty-ordered course with unlearned lessons), a `KanaStepRow` "Step 00" acknowledging kana as a silent prerequisite (JapaneseRoadmapPage.tsx:90-115), a Daily Loop of three parallel tiles — review / next lesson / immerse (`RoadmapDailyLoop.tsx`) — and an honest "Path clear" fallback once all 24 steps are cleared (RoadmapDailyLoop.tsx:42-44). `SetupChecklist.tsx` front-loads the six offline packs the section depends on (JMdict, a frequency dict, KANJIDIC, KRADFILE, grammar library, Tatoeba sentences) with live installed-state and unlocks-what copy, and disappears once done (lines 76-77) rather than nagging forever.

So: yes, a coherent path exists — this is not a pile of unrelated tools. But it is a path with two structurally separate halves that don't talk to each other well: the SRS/curriculum half (roadmap, review, quiz) and the comprehension/reading half (coverage %, i+1 feed, prep deck) are bridged only by `jp_card.front` matching, and — per the already-established coverageRepo facts plus the "known fires in 11 minutes" finding above — the reading half's numbers are noisy in BOTH directions: pessimistic before a deck exists (prior knowledge invisible), optimistic the moment cards start graduating (freshly-introduced words count the same as year-old ones).

## Versus established method

- **Anki/Yomitan sentence mining**: NaviHUB's mining flow (2-4 clicks, auto-enters SRS, offline dictionary autofill) matches Yomitan's ergonomics for the JAPANESE half but drops two of its three pillars — sentence cards (every mined card is word-primary; see Mining) and audio (video-only). Duplicate detection is UI-only, not enforced (Mining, finding 2).
- **WaniKani-style radicals→kanji→vocab ordering**: the section HAS the ingredients (`Kanji Radicals & Components` course, KRADFILE-backed build-a-kanji/lookalike drills) but the roadmap's 1-24 sequence is grammar/vocab/kanji-course-interleaved by JLPT level, not a strict components-first gate — a deliberate difference (JLPT-shaped, not WaniKani-shaped), reasonable for a learner whose goal is reading rather than kanji mastery per se.
- **Graded readers / tadoku (extensive reading at ~95-98% known-word density)**: this is the weakest link. `coverageForMedia`'s projection ("learn the top N words → coverage becomes X%", coverageRepo.ts:129-163) is a real jpdb-style feature and a good idea, but it is undermined by the "known" tier firing on freshly-graduated cards (Scheduler, finding 1) — a learner could watch their coverage % climb toward the tadoku-recommended threshold on cards that haven't actually stuck yet, then hit a wall reading the real book because "known" over-promised.
- **i+1 comprehensible input**: `jpFeed.buildFeed`'s two-phase coarse/exact design (already established) is methodologically sound MorphMan-style engineering, and the honest empty-state when the deck is small is the right failure mode. It inherits the same known-tier optimism as coverage.
- **kotu-style pitch minimal pairs**: `MinimalPairsDrill` is a straight, honest port of the real mechanic (real recordings, real contour choices). `SpeakDrill`'s local YIN-based production feedback is the standout technical achievement of the whole vertical — a genuinely rare feature for an offline single-user app — and it is the most epistemically honest drill in the section about what it can and can't measure.

Net: NaviHUB is a real tool for the SRS/curriculum half (scheduler correctness issues aside, it's a working Anki clone with real content behind it) and for isolated skill drills (pitch, keigo, transitivity, dictation are all legitimate, non-gameable exercises). It is closer to a toy for the "am I ready to read this" question specifically, because the one signal that answers that (coverage/known-word tiering) is built on a definition of "known" that's honest about cold-start (established) but not about warm-start optimism (this review).

## The walls at week 2 / month 2 / month 6

- **Week 2**: most of the wave-2/3 drills (pitch, homophone, loanword, names, grammar cloze, dictation, minimal pairs, the i+1 feed) are hard-gated behind six separately-downloaded offline packs (`SetupChecklist.tsx:36-74`). `SetupChecklist` does surface this well on the hub, but a learner who skips it or doesn't recognize "KANJIDIC"/"a frequency dictionary" hits `EmptyState` after `EmptyState` across most of the section's breadth — the SRS/curriculum half still works, but "the section" feels much smaller than it is until that one-time setup is done.
- **Month 2**: the review backlog and the comprehension score move in opposite directions from what the learner expects. At the default `newLimit` of 10/day (JapaneseReviewPage.tsx:240-249) with no daily time-budget estimate anywhere in the UI, review load compounds (nothing paces new-card introduction against review capacity — `introducedToday` is advisory only, JapaneseReviewPage.tsx:250-259) while `coverageForMedia` on the learner's actual target manga is still low because the deck is still small relative to a real series' vocabulary (the already-established near-zero-at-cold-start problem). Two months of visible SRS progress (streaks, cleared roadmap steps) doesn't yet translate into "I can read this," which is the actual stated goal.
- **Month 6**: at a sustainable pace the ~2,130-card seeded curriculum (24 steps) is close to exhausted — the authored path has a defined end, honestly acknowledged by "Path clear" (RoadmapDailyLoop.tsx:42-44). Past that point the app hands off entirely to self-directed, un-paced tools: one 500-word core-deck dump (`coreDeck.ts:27`, no follow-up schedule) and per-book prep decks (`prepDeck.ts:204`, same). Nothing in the roadmap models "read book 1, mine as you go, prep-deck book 2 when ready" as a repeating loop — RoadmapDailyLoop's tile 03 (Immerse) does point at whatever manga is in-progress or the i+1 feed, which is the right instinct, but the mining-inbox ordering bug (Mining, finding 1) means immersion-driven vocabulary is structurally deprioritized against a curriculum that, by month 6, is largely already cleared.

## What's genuinely good here

- **`SpeakDrill` + `shared/pitchTrack.ts`** is a from-scratch YIN pitch tracker with honest, UI-surfaced limits (odaka≡heiban in isolation, devoiced-mora "unclear" instead of a wrong mark, shape-only framing stated in the drill's own setup copy at `SpeakDrill.tsx:216-219`) — a real technical accomplishment for an offline single-user app, not a toy.
- **The seeded curriculum is not filler**: 24 courses, difficulty 1-24 with zero gaps, ~2,130 hand-authored cards, and a dedicated `Manga & VN Japanese` course (japaneseSeed.ts:725-919) that specifically targets the contractions, particles and casual speech that textbooks skip and manga runs on — directly matched to this user's stated goal.
- **`shared/typing.ts:matchState`'s invariant** ("never 'wrong' while any accepted answer is still reachable by typing more") is a genuinely rigorous piece of engineering for per-keystroke drills, and it's shared cleanly across kana, numbers and any future typed drill.
- **`SetupChecklist.tsx`** is a well-designed onboarding nudge: live installed-state mirrored from the real Settings predicates, explains what each pack unlocks, and disappears once done instead of nagging forever.
- **The confusable-distractor machinery** (`shared/confusables.ts`, `shared/similarKanji.ts`, `jpConfusables.ts`) generates wrong answers that are REAL confusables (transitivity partners, homophones, component-overlap lookalikes) rather than random pool members — this is uncommon care for an auto-generated MC quiz and it's what keeps most of the drill table above rated "not defeatable."
- **`jpFeed.buildFeed`'s two-phase design** (coarse keyword prefilter → exact kuromoji pass) correctly reasons about a real correctness trap (base/surface conflation in the FTS index) that would have been easy to miss, and is regression-tested for it.
